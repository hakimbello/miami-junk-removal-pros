The Problem With Airbnb Turnovers in Miami

Miami hosts operate under some of the tightest turnaround windows in the country. Back-to-back bookings are the norm, not the exception — especially during peak season from December through April. The standard window is 11AM checkout, 3PM check-in. Four hours to transform a lived-in unit into a guest-ready space that earns five-star cleanliness reviews.

That window is already tight for cleaning alone. It becomes impossible when the previous guest left more than normal wear behind. A broken beach chair on the balcony. A cooler full of melted ice in the closet. Food rotting in the refrigerator. A mattress stained beyond what fresh linens can fix. Furniture that was never supposed to be in the unit. Trash bags piled in corners that exceed what a standard turnover covers.

A regular cleaning company will clean around those items — or refuse the job entirely. They do not haul furniture. They do not remove broken appliances. They do not take away the kayak someone left on the patio. So the host calls a second vendor — a junk removal company — and tries to coordinate two separate arrivals in a four-hour window. The cleaner shows up at noon but the hauler is scheduled for 2PM. The unit is not guest-ready at 3PM. The next guest arrives to a space that is still being worked on, or worse, partially cleaned with junk still sitting in the living room.

One vendor being late throws off the entire turnover. One miscommunication about scope leaves the host scrambling. One bad turnover costs a five-star review, a Superhost badge, and bookings for weeks. Miami hosts deserve a better system — and that is exactly what we built.
